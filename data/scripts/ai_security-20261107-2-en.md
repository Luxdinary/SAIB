## Scene 1 (hook)
The strongest password in your company is worthless the moment an employee types it into the wrong page.
> visual: headline="The password on the wrong page" kind="hook"

## Scene 2 (card)
That is what passkeys are for. According to the FIDO Alliance, a passkey is an authentication credential based on FIDO standards. It is stored on your device and unlocked the way you unlock that device: fingerprint, face or PIN. [S1]
> visual: headline="What a passkey is" body="A FIDO-standard credential, stored on the device, unlocked like the device" kind="card"

## Scene 3 (card)
The difference from a password: passkeys are, in the words of the FIDO Alliance, always strong and phishing-resistant, because there is no shared secret to type in or steal. A fake login page gets nothing. [S1]
> visual: headline="No shared secret" body="Nothing to type, nothing to steal" kind="card"

## Scene 4 (fact)
The stated goal: the passkey replaces the password as the first factor for account authentication. The FIDO Alliance calls it the path to passwordless authentication. [S1]
> visual: headline="A replacement for the password" number="1" body="Passkey instead of password as the first factor" kind="fact"

## Scene 5 (card)
Where passkeys are not available yet, multi-factor authentication stays mandatory. The UK National Cyber Security Centre has its own guidance for corporate online services and recommends techniques that give better protection against phishing. [S2]
> visual: headline="Otherwise: MFA, phishing-resistant" body="NCSC guidance for corporate online services" kind="card"

## Scene 6 (payoff)
Today: check whether your company email, bank and cloud storage offer passkeys, and turn them on there first. More protection for small businesses: follow.
> visual: headline="Today: email, bank, cloud" kind="payoff"
